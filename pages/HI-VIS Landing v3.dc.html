02 — WHY WE'RE BUILDING THIS

TWO PROBLEMS, ONE MODEL.

The numbers on the last slide are a policy failure, not a run of bad luck. On the ground it splits into two separate gaps — and one model closes both.
SAFETY
COVERAGE IS THIN AND INCONSISTENT.

PPE checks on site are a spot inspection by one person, walking round once a day. Everything that happens between rounds goes unchecked.

EVIDENCE
THE RECORDS ALREADY EXIST. THEY'RE JUST NOT RECORDS.

Poor contemporaneous records are the most common weakness in construction claims. Photos exist by the thousand — none of them indexed, tagged, or searchable.

PPE
PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT
The safety kit every person on a site is required to wear. Five items — that is what the model looks for on each person it finds.
■ WORN — logged ■ MISSING — flagged
Every person in every photo gets these five checked, and the result is dated and kept.

05 — AND WE TRIED AN LLM TOO

COULD A GENERAL-PURPOSE LLM DO THIS TOO?

Every model above is purpose-built — it only ever sees the PPE classes it was trained on. Before committing further to a custom pipeline, we wanted to see how far an off-the-shelf, general-purpose LLM could get on the same task with no fine-tuning at all: given a construction-site photo, can it read PPE compliance straight out of the box? We ran the same demo photos through several vision-capable LLMs, side by side, to see how they'd stack up against our own model.

07 — ONE MORE QUESTION
MODEL SHIPPED. THE NUMBERS SAID WE WERE DONE. WE WERE NOT CONVINCED.
We didn't have time to relabel everything — so we hand-relabelled 900 photos with a cleaner, unified schema, ran them through preprocessing, and grew that into a 26,500-image dataset. Then we retrained and pulled up the metrics.
